Teachers have two big federal forgiveness routes, and they don’t combine neatly. Picking the wrong one can leave real money on the table. An AI calculator is useful here because the comparison is mostly arithmetic: what you pay under each path, and how long it takes.

What is the best student loan strategy for teachers?

Teachers can use Teacher Loan Forgiveness (up to $17,500 or $5,000 after five years at a low-income school) or Public Service Loan Forgiveness (after 120 qualifying payments). The same years can’t count toward both. Smaller balances often suit Teacher Loan Forgiveness. Larger balances often suit PSLF.

Same years for both?NoNo

The larger TLF amount, $17,500, goes to full-time secondary math or science teachers and special education teachers. Other qualifying teachers can receive $5,000. Your school has to be listed in the federal low-income school directory for the years you claim, and it’s worth checking the list before you accept a job. The Teacher Loan Forgiveness page has the details.

An example: $38,000 at 6%

A made-up teacher owes $38,000 at 6%. The standard 10-year payment is $421.88, and the full repayment costs $50,625. We’ll compare four paths. For the PSLF path I assume her income stays at an AGI of $52,000, which places her in the 5% band of the RAP plan, so the payment is about $216.67. In real life, raises would push it up, so treat this path as a best case.

For a math or special education teacher, the $17,500 route cleared the loan in under six years at about $29,761. The PSLF route could cost less in total, but it needs ten years in a qualifying job and her payments would likely rise with salary, narrowing the gap. For a teacher at the $5,000 tier, PSLF looks much better on these numbers. The key point is that the answer depends on your tier, your balance, how long you plan to teach, and how fast your pay grows.

A rough decision guide

Your situationWhat often fits
Balance under $17,500 and you teach secondary math, science or special educationTeacher Loan Forgiveness may clear most or all of it
Balance $17,500 to $30,000Compare both; TLF may be faster
Balance above about $40,000 and you plan to teach 10+ yearsPSLF is usually worth modelling first
You expect to leave teaching after 5 or 6 yearsTLF, because PSLF needs 10 years
Your school is not on the low-income listPSLF only, if the employer qualifies

This is a rule of thumb, not an answer. Run your own numbers.

Other teacher-specific programs to check

ProgramWorth knowing
Perkins loan cancellationTeachers at low-income schools or in certain subjects can have Perkins loans cancelled over time. Don’t consolidate Perkins loans without checking.
TEACH GrantConverts to a loan if you don’t finish the service requirement, so confirm you completed it.
State and district programsMany states run their own loan help for teachers in high-need areas. Ask your district.
Employer assistanceSome districts add a benefit toward student loans. Ask HR.

Common mistakes

MistakeFix
Assuming the school qualifiesCheck the directory for each year you teach
Applying for TLF without thinking about PSLFThose five years can’t count for PSLF afterward, so model both first
Consolidating without a planIt can change payment counts and plan options. See the consolidation guide
Forgetting to certify employmentSubmit PSLF forms yearly using the official PSLF Help Tool

Limits of this guide: rules and school lists change, and a calculator can’t verify your school or your loan type. Confirm everything on StudentAid.gov and with your servicer. Don’t pay anyone to file these applications, since both programs are free to apply for.

Borrowing in India? Teacher-specific US forgiveness doesn’t apply to Indian education loans. Ask your bank about interest subsidy or priority-sector rules for the course you studied.
