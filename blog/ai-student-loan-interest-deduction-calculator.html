Most borrowers know their student loan has an interest rate. Fewer know that part of the interest they pay can lower their tax bill. An AI student loan interest deduction calculator won’t file your return, but it will tell you roughly what the deduction is worth before you open the tax software.

How does the student loan interest deduction work?

You can deduct the lesser of $2,500 or the interest you actually paid on a qualified student loan, whether or not you itemise. It lowers your taxable income, not your tax directly. The deduction shrinks and then disappears as your income rises, and married people filing separately can’t claim it.

What you need before you start

  • Interest paid, not payments made. Only the interest part counts. Your servicer sends Form 1098-E if you paid $600 or more in interest in the year. Below $600 you can still claim it, using the servicer’s online statement.
  • Your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI). Roughly your AGI before this deduction.
  • Your filing status and your top tax rate, to turn the deduction into dollars.

The loan must be in your name, and nobody can claim you as a dependent. A student can’t claim interest on a Parent PLUS loan, because the parent is the borrower.

Example 1: the deduction fits under the cap

Say a made-up borrower, Ravi, owes $26,000 at 6.8% on a 10-year plan. His payment is $299.21, and in year one about $1,710 of his payments is interest. If his income is below the phase-out, he deducts $1,710. At a 22% tax rate that cuts his tax by about $376.

Example 2: the cap kicks in

Now take a $52,000 loan at 7.4%. Interest in year one is roughly $3,726, but only $2,500 is deductible. At a 24% rate, that’s a saving of $600. The extra $1,226 of interest you paid gets no tax relief at all. This is why a bigger loan costs more than the headline number suggests.

Example 3: the phase-out

For the 2025 tax year, the deduction phases out for single filers between $85,000 and $100,000 of MAGI, and for joint filers between $170,000 and $200,000. Those thresholds are adjusted over time, so confirm the current ones in IRS Publication 970. Suppose a single filer has MAGI of $90,000. That’s a third of the way through the $15,000 range, so the deduction falls by a third:

Don’t double count employer payments

If your employer pays toward your loan under a tax-free benefit (up to $5,250 a year), you can’t deduct the same interest. Only interest you paid yourself counts. Our graduate toolkit explains how to ask for that benefit.

Where it goes on your return

The deduction is entered on Schedule 1 of Form 1040, which carries it to your adjustments to income. A tax program asks for the 1098-E figure and does the rest. The IRS explains the rules in Topic 456. Ask a preparer if you have more than one filer, self-employment income or a big income change.

What AI should not do here: decide your filing status, estimate your MAGI from a guess, or tell you what to put on a return. It’s good at the arithmetic once you give it the numbers. The tax decision is yours or your preparer’s. Keep your SSN and account numbers out of the chat.

Borrowing in India? The nearest equivalent is the deduction for interest on an education loan under Section 80E of the old Income Tax Act. The new Income Tax Act has renumbered sections and the new default tax regime may not allow it, so check the current rule on incometax.gov.in.
